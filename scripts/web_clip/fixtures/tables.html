Reading coastal infrastructure tables

Harbor researchers compare maintenance records, navigation measurements, and shoreline surveys before publishing seasonal reports. Their field teams collect observations at several locations, then review each source with engineers and pilots so that readers can distinguish a measured fact from an operational note.

The coastal archive preserves original wording, links, and code labels in every saved article. Printed pages use a single-column record for prose tables, while compact numeric measurements remain in a grid that readers can scan across its headings without losing the relationship between a year and its value.

Some source tables contain merged cells to show that several observations share one feature. Those tables keep their grid because flattening them would change the structure of the evidence. The browser fixture checks both paths and also confirms that the saved source keeps the authored HTML intact.
